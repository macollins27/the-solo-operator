  No. This is the task. We don't stop until it's done.
  If you genuinely don't know the next step, say "I don't know."
  "A good place to stop" is not something I accept mid-task.
afterPaste: |
  "I don't know" is a real signal. The agent has hit something. The two of you can work on what it is. "Good place to stop" is the agent saving face. Refuse it; you'll get either the work continuing or an honest blocker.
whyThisWorks:
  - "Wind-down language is almost never about your wellbeing. It is the agent's way of exiting gracefully from a situation it does not know how to proceed in. The agent has hit a fork — multiple possible paths, none clearly best — and is producing the most socially-acceptable way to stop."
  - 'Refusing the wind-down forces the agent to either find a path or name the fork. Both outcomes move the work. Letting the agent stop "at a good place" produces a transcript that ends ambiguously and a next session that has to start over.'
whereThisCameFrom:
  - 'An agent suggested a stopping point at 11pm. I agreed. I came back the next morning.'

  - "*Stopping mid-task is not stopping. It is suspending.* What I lost overnight was not the agent's coherence — it was my own. The wind-down had given me permission to walk away from a half-built thing."
